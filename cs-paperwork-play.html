The paperwork play

  • Client OFW manpower agency, PH
  • Markets NZ · AU · UAE · JP
  • Engagement Sep 2022 – Nov 2025
  • Scope Blog, landing pages, GTM

4Overseas markets built and tracked at once

The situation

The client places Filipino workers with employers abroad, and was asked to build organic visibility across four overseas markets at once. The direct path — rank for “recruitment agency”, “hiring agency”, “manpower agency” once per country — runs straight into a ceiling. These are low-frequency, B2B-flavoured searches: even a first-page ranking for “philippines recruitment agency” reaches an estimated 50 searches a month.

The bet

The audience that actually needs this agency is enormous: every Overseas Filipino Worker navigating the bureaucracy of working abroad — POEA registration, passport appointments, visa and contract requirements. None of that is a recruitment keyword. All of it is searched by exactly the people the agency wants to reach, at volumes the branded terms will never touch.

So the engagement bet on the second audience: two evergreen guides a week on the practical paperwork of overseas work, paired with country-specific landing pages built to catch whoever that content brought in.

◆ When the category has no volume, go one step earlier. The paperwork every applicant has to file first has no such ceiling.
The build

Content first. The country pages had to earn their spot

The brief started narrow — blog posts only — and widened twice as the writing proved it could carry commercial weight.

SEP 2022

Foundation

Hired to write evergreen blog content only, two posts a week, with rank tracking set up per target country so movement could be measured market by market.

2023

Earning the mandate

The writing performed well enough on the blog to extend into the site’s commercial core: landing page content for each country’s Employer’s Corner.

2024

A named GTM push

Market research rolled into a formal go-to-market initiative — “Tracing Spaces, Bridging Gaps” — positioning the agency’s OFW manpower services for an international audience.

The result

Three years of compounding, one seasonal curve

Site-wide organic clicks went from a rounding error in 2022 to 142,847 across the engagement, riding a curve shaped by OFW application seasons rather than any single viral moment.

Every year traces the same shape: a climb into application season, a plateau, a pullback. The site did not just get bigger — it learned the calendar of the audience it was built for.

The route map

One content hub, four markets, four different receipts

Every country got the same page template fed by the same guide content — but not the same language. English is the working language of business, and of the OFW audience itself, in Australia, the UAE and New Zealand. Japan was not, and there was no budget for a native-speaking editor.

What this chart is showing Monthly organic visits per country landing page, not conversions. The three English-language markets and the machine-translated one are separated deliberately: the gap tracks the language decision at least as closely as it tracks anything about the markets themselves.

The real engine

The guide nobody asked the agency to write

While the recruitment-agency pages fought over double-digit search volumes, a quick guide to the POEA e-registration process — a mandatory step for any Filipino applying to work abroad — pulled 470 clicks a month on its own. Nineteen per cent of everything the entire site earns.

Three of the site’s four best-performing pages are process guides, not sales pages. This is the same content logic as the country landing pages, aimed one step earlier in the journey: meet the worker before they have chosen an agency, while they are still solving the paperwork in front of them.

Why it worked

Three principles behind the curve

Two of these are about where to aim. The third is the one that cost the engagement a market.

01 / VOLUME

Go one step earlier

“Recruitment agency” tops out around 50 to 140 searches a month per market. The paperwork every applicant has to file first has no such ceiling, and the same people are doing both.

02 / TRUST

Content earns access before credit

The brief was blog posts only. The mandate to write the commercial landing pages — the ones that actually sell — came after the evergreen content had proved it could perform.

03 / LANGUAGE

A translated page is not a localised one

Japan ranked — top ten on ten terms — and still drew the fewest visits of the four Employer’s Corner pages. Ranking and reading are different problems: the template was auto-translated with no native editor to catch what did not land. Four markets needed four language budgets, not one template.
